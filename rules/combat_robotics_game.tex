\section{Introduction}

\textbf{Robot Battles} is a game about combat robotics. Build a robot within your budget and weight limit, then defeat your opponents in a tournament of destruction.

\section{Card Anatomy}

Cards represent the physical components, electronics, mechanical assemblies, and weapons installed onto your robot chassis.

The anatomy of each card consists of the following key attributes:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=!, labelwidth=4.2cm, font=\bfseries\color{primaryDark}]
  \item[Price (\textcolor{costgreen}{\textbf{\$X}}):] You cannot have more total price in your pool than your budget.
  \item[Weight (\inlineIcon{\weighticonplain}):] Your robot's total weight must be within the weight budget.
  \item[Requirements (\inlineChevron\inlineIcon{\resourceE}):] A component is only active if its requirements are supplied by connected components. (Power, spin, pressure are the resources that need supply.)
  \item[Durability (\inlineIcon{\durabilityicon}):] How much damage a component can take before destruction.
  \item[Absorption (\inlineIcon{\absorptionicon}):] How much damage a component absorbs to reduce throw strength received during an attack (also absorbs feedback damage).
  \item[Outputs (\inlineIcon{\resourceS}\inlineChevron):] What resources the component supplies. Also includes drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM}.
  \item[Ability Text:] Any special rules or passive traits for the component.
\end{description}

\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{Weapon Cards:} Weapon cards will have weapon damage \inlineIcon{\resourceW} in their output zone, and a template marker indicating which template the component uses and where it attacks (Figure~\ref{fig:card_weapon}).

\section{Terminology \& Keywords}

The cards that make up the robots are called components.

\subsection{Resource Types}

Components produce and require various resource types. The primary resources used in the game are:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{c l X}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Icon} & \textbf{Name} & \textbf{Description} \\
  \midrule
  \inlineIcon{\resourceE} & \textbf{Energy}   & Electrical power generated by batteries, used to power motors and servos. \\
  \inlineIcon{\resourceS} & \textbf{Spin}     & Rotation produced by motors and consumed by weapons and wheels. \\
  \inlineIcon{\resourceP} & \textbf{Pressure} & Pneumatic power for weapons. \\
  \inlineIcon{\resourceM} & \textbf{Drive} \inlineIcon{\resourceM}    & Motive power of the vehicle. This defines how fast your robot can move. \\
  \inlineIcon{\resourceW} & \textbf{Weapon Damage} \inlineIcon{\resourceW}   & The strength of the weapon impact. \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\subsection{Keywords}

Components may have keywords that define specific mechanical rules and interactions:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.10}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{p{3.8cm} X}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Keyword} & \textbf{Summary} \\
  \midrule
  \keywordbadge{kw_fragile}{Fragile} & If this component takes weapon damage \inlineIcon{\resourceW} it is destroyed as if it had a durability of 1 \\
  \keywordbadge{kw_spin_up}{Spin up X} & Add a spin counter at turn start (max X); cleared on attack, hitting a wall or becoming inactive. \\
  \keywordbadge{kw_forks}{Forks} & If the opposing robot is in line with the forks during contact, they become raised for this contact (does not work while inverted)  \\
  \keywordbadge{kw_wedge}{Wedge} & If this component makes contact, the opponent becomes raised for this contact and this component has double absorption against weapon contact (does not work while inverted or raised) \\
  \keywordbadge{kw_invertible}{Invertible} & Component functions normally while inverted. \\
  \keywordbadge{kw_self_right}{Self-right} & Inverts upright at turn end without contact if active. \\
  \keywordbadge{kw_reset}{Reset} & This weapon is inactive the turn after it is used. (this does not cause the robot to be considered knocked out if it is also a self righter) \\
  \keywordbadge{kw_overhead}{Overhead} & This weapon hits a random component along the contact line instead of the outermost. Does not cause a throw. \\
  \keywordbadge{kw_trigger}{Trigger} & If an opposing weapon makes contact this weapon does not attack. \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\section{Robot Construction}

Each player first had to build their robot. The total weight of the robot must be
within the weight limit (40) and the total cost of components in the robot and the supply
must be within the budget (\$50).

The robot's construction will determine how fast it can move, what damage it can deal and
what damage it can take and still work. 

Each robot is built onto a chassis. A chassis is a large mat that shows the robot's shape and has a weight, cost, and flip strength. It may have an ability. All cards on the robot must be on the chassis.

The chassis determines the model used for play, and all components must be within the bounds of the chassis.

A card is considered connected to any other card it is touching. Cards may overlap, but each car must be clearly visible.


You may submit up to two robots (e.g., clusterbots); the total weight across all robots must be within the weight limit.
A clusterbot team is knocked out only when both robots are knocked out.
Each robot must have active drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} (that is, active components that supply drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM}) to be submitted.


\subsection{Resource Supply and Split Supply}

A component is only active if its requirements are supplied by connected components.
Components can supply and be supplied by multiple components, but the total amount a component can supply is it's output.

A component only supplies downstream components if it is active, and it is only active if it has it's requirememts supplied.

When there is not enough supply for all connected components, the player chooses which components get supplied during the planning phase each turn.

\section{Battle Rules}

\subsection{Match Length \& Win Conditions}

The battle is played for \textbf{20 rounds} or until a robot is knocked out. A robot is knocked out if it has no active drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} and no way to uninvert itself to regain drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} (a self-righting weapon temporarily inactive due to \textit{Reset} does not cause the robot to be considered knocked out). A robot pushed or thrown into a marked Hazard Wall is also immediately knocked out.

If neither robot is knocked out by the end of round 20, the winner is decided by a \textbf{Judge's Decision}. The judge's decision damage count is the number of cards destroyed followed by the number of cards damaged (the robot with fewer cards destroyed wins; if tied, the robot with fewer cards damaged wins; if still tied, the match is a draw).

\subsection{Round Structure}

Each round follows a sequence of 3 steps:

\noindent
{\small\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l p{12.5cm}}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Step} & \textbf{Summary} \\
  \midrule
  \textbf{1. Planning} & Players secretly select drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} values for each side and choose which components to supply. \\
  \textbf{2. Movement} & Choices are simultaneously revealed; robots advance along the movement templates. \\
  \textbf{3. Collision} & Collisions and contacts (active weapon hits or inert pushes) are resolved. \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabularx}}


\subsection{Planning}
In the planning step the player chooses how their robot is going to move this round.
They each secretly choose a number for the left and right side of their robot: the drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} values.
These numbers can be any whole number between plus/minus the sum of all active drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} on that side. (that is, drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} output by active components on that side of the robot)

The bigger the number the more forwards that side of the robot goes. 
The bigger the difference the more the robot will turn.

\subsection{Movement}

Movement units are found on the straight movement template. Where an effect says a robot moves that amount, move them along that template without changing facing.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{images/movement_templates.png}
    \caption{Movement Templates}
    \label{fig:movement_templates}
\end{figure}

Both robots advance along the template corresponding to their chosen drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} values.

Movement details:
\begingroup
\emergencystretch=1.5em
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*., leftmargin=*, noitemsep, topsep=0.5pt]
      \item \textbf{Forward Alignment \& Stop Lines:} Front bumper starts flush at black baseline. On curved templates, target stop lines are drawn at the robot's intended bumper angle; align the bumper flush against the target line to achieve the correct position and facing.
      \item \textbf{Left/Right Symmetry (Flipping):} Templates are printed for right turns (where left drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} is greater than or equal to right drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM}). If turning left (where right drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} is greater than left drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM}), flip the template face-down.
      \item \textbf{Reverse Maneuvers (Rear Placement):} If drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} is negative, place the template baseline against the \textbf{back} (rear) of the robot; move backward to the target line.
      \item \textbf{Asymmetric Drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} with a Negative:} When drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} choices have opposite signs with unequal magnitudes (for example, \texttt{3/\allowbreak-1} or \texttt{3/\allowbreak-2}), resolve the movement in two steps: first spin on the spot using the Spin Disc for the shared magnitude, then from that new heading use the Pivot Fan for the remainder.
      \item \textbf{Movements Beyond the Template:} If drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} exceeds template markings (for example, \texttt{5/0}, \texttt{5/-5}, or boosted drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM}), move to the template end, then place another template for the remainder.
      \item \textbf{Path Overlap \& Collision Check:} Overlapping paths cause collisions at contact.
\end{enumerate}
\endgroup

\subsubsection{Movement Examples}

\vspace{0.2em}
\noindent\textbf{1. Straight Driving and Reverse Maneuvers (\texttt{3/3} vs \texttt{-2/-2})}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, noitemsep, topsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{(\texttt{3/3}):} Align Straight Template baseline flush against the \textbf{front bumper}. Advance straight forward until the front bumper aligns with the blue \textbf{L3 R3} line. Heading is unchanged.
  \item \textbf{(\texttt{-2/-2}):} Negative drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} moves backward. Align baseline flush against the \textbf{rear bumper} pointing backward. Reverse until the rear bumper aligns with \textbf{L2 R2}. Heading is unchanged.

  \item \textbf{(\texttt{1/2}):} Turning left. Take the Medium Curve Template and \textbf{flip it face-down}. Advance along the arc to line \textbf{L2 R1}, sweeping counter-clockwise.

\noindent\textbf{3. Pivot Fan vs. Spin Disc Rotations (\texttt{2/0} vs \texttt{2/-2})}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, noitemsep, topsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{(\texttt{2/0}):} Right wheel is locked. Place Pivot Fan red marker on the \textbf{right wheel}. Robot swings clockwise around that wheel by 90 degrees (two 45-degree steps) to line \textbf{L2 R0}.
  \item \textbf{(\texttt{2/-2}):} Tracks counter-rotate equally. Center the Spin Disc under the chassis. Robot rotates 180 degrees clockwise (two 90-degree steps) to line \textbf{L2 R-2} on the spot with zero displacement.
\end{itemize}

\section{Collisions \& Contact Resolution}

\subsection{Determining the Contact Point}

If the robots paths intersect then there is a collision at that point. 
For a collision, place both robots as far along their path as they can go without touching the other's path, then slowly move both forwards until they touch each other. Where the robots touch shows the contact point. Align the contact line from the template with the chassis and follow along that line into the robot's card layout to determine which component is contacted. Where ambiguous, both components make contact.

If the parts of either robot that is touching is the weapon's active area (the red zone on the template), then the contact is active.
 Otherwise, it is an inert contact.

\subsection{Inert Contact (Pushing Match)}

Essentially a pushing match between colliding chassis surfaces:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Move along templates until contact.
  \item Displace the remainder of robot A's template to the end of robot B's template.
  \item Move both robots along the straight line from the initial contact point to the end of A's displaced template (a raised robot's motion does not contribute).
  \item If a robot is pushed into a wall, it is thrown with throw strength equal to the largest drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} value the other robot chose. If its weapon contacts the wall, it loses all spin counters and takes feedback equal to half the sum of its weapon strength and throw strength, rounded down. The robot takes throw damage equal to the throw strength.
  \item If the robots are still touching, both robots are moved back a full unit to separate them.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Active Contact (Weapon Hits)}

When weapons collide with an opponent's chassis or components:
\begin{itemize}
  \item All weapons involved in the contact are assumed to hit, and they deal weapon damage \inlineIcon{\resourceW} according to their damage level to the single outermost component contacted (damage does not penetrate inwards), unless a weapon has \textbf{Overhead} (which hits a random component along the contact line instead of the outermost and causes no throw) or \textbf{Trigger} (which does not attack if an opposing weapon makes contact).
  \item Then both robots are thrown away from the contact. The base throw strength received is equal to the weapon damage \inlineIcon{\resourceW} minus the absorption of the hit component (minimum 0). Any additions to throw strength are added after that.
  \item Thrown distance is the roll of a six-sided die or the throw strength, whichever is lower. Thrown robot orientation after active contact is decided by rolling a die and rotating that many 60-degree steps clockwise (using the numbered ticks on the rim of the Spin Disc template).
  \item Weapons receive feedback equal to half the combined attack strength in the contact. A weapon contacting an arena wall loses all spin counters and takes feedback equal to half the sum of its weapon strength and throw strength, rounded down.
  \item If after active contact the robots are still in contact, proceed with inertial contact.
\end{itemize}

\begin{summarybox}{Contact Classification Quick Reference}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l X}
  \textbf{Contact Type} & \textbf{Resolution Mechanism} \\
  \midrule
  \textbf{Active Contact} & Weapon strikes component $\rightarrow$ Deals weapon damage \inlineIcon{\resourceW} to outermost component contacted $\rightarrow$ Base throw strength equals weapon damage \inlineIcon{\resourceW} minus absorption of hit component (minimum 0) plus additions $\rightarrow$ Weapons take feedback equal to half combined attack strength $\rightarrow$ Thrown distance is the lower of the die roll or the throw strength $\rightarrow$ Thrown robot orientation rotates clockwise by that many 60-degree steps as rolled on the die $\rightarrow$ Throw damage equals throw strength. If robots are still in contact, proceed with inertial contact. \\
  \addlinespace[3pt]
  \textbf{Inert Contact} & Pushing match $\rightarrow$ Displace remainder of A's template to end of B's $\rightarrow$ Move along straight line from contact to end of displaced template $\rightarrow$ Wall collision causes throw (takes throw damage equal to throw strength) $\rightarrow$ If still touching, move both back a full unit to separate them. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{summarybox}

\subsection{The Ground Game}

Wedges and forks can both inflict the raised status on opponents when they hit. A raised robot does not contribute any motion to the pushing in inert contact. It receives an additional throw strength of 2 after any active contact (even if it is the only one whose weapon hit) and its attacks do not throw.

When both robots would be raised in the same contact, randomly choose one by rolling a dice: that one is raised and the other isn't.

\section{Combat Resolution, Damage \& Status Effects}

\subsection{Throws \& Inversion}

A robot that is thrown is moved a distance equal to the lower of a die roll or the throw strength away from the contact point along the center-to-center line.
Robot orientation after active contact is decided by rolling a die and rotating that many 60-degree steps clockwise (using the numbered ticks on the rim of the Spin Disc template).
If the throw strength minus the thrown distance is greater than the flip value of the chassis, the robot is \textbf{inverted} (lower rolled distances represent high-arching vertical throws rather than horizontal displacement).
After the throw, the thrown robot takes throw damage at a randomly chosen component equal to the throw strength.
If the robot is pushed or thrown into a marked \textbf{Hazard Wall}, it is \textbf{immediately knocked out}.
The component is chosen randomly by dropping a die onto the robot and seeing which component it lands on.

\subsection{Damage Resolution}

Weapon damage \inlineIcon{\resourceW} applies only to the single outermost component contacted (internal components are damaged via throw shock):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Component Destroyed:} A component is destroyed if the damage level that hits it is equal to or greater than its durability.
  \item \textbf{Component Damaged:} A component is damaged if the damage level that hits it is equal to or greater than half its durability.
  \item \textbf{Repeated Damage:} If a damaged component would be damaged again, it is instead destroyed.
  \item \textbf{Absorption on Destruction:} If a component is destroyed, it absorbs damage equal to its durability instead of its absorption.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Feedback}

Feedback represents components over-straining themselves under combat loads. It is damage that progresses back from the source component 
to each component that supplies it, shared equally if there are multiple components (rounding up), down the tree until the damage is all absorbed.

\subsection{Status Effects}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=!, labelwidth=2.5cm, font=\bfseries\color{primaryDark}]
  \item[Inverted:] While a robot is inverted, weapons, drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM}, and component abilities (such as wedges and forks) without the \textit{invertible} keyword don't work. If an inverted robot would be inverted again, it becomes the right way up.
  \item[Raised:] Inflicted by wedges or forks during contact. A raised robot does not contribute any motion to pushing in inert contact, loses the double absorption of its wedges, its attacks do not cause a throw, and it receives an additional throw strength of 2 after active contact. Its flip strength is also halved.
\end{description}

\begin{summarybox}{Damage \& Feedback Rules Summary}
\small
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=1.5pt, topsep=1pt, parsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Direct Weapon Damage \inlineIcon{\resourceW}:} Weapon damage \inlineIcon{\resourceW} applies only to the single outermost component hit; excess damage does not penetrate inwards (throw shock covers internals).
  \item \textbf{Damage Conditions:} A component is \textbf{Destroyed} if it takes damage equal to or greater than its durability, and \textbf{Damaged} if it takes damage equal to or greater than half its durability. Absorption reduces base throw strength received during attacks.
  \item \textbf{Throw Distance \& Damage:} Thrown distance is the lower of a die roll or the throw strength. Thrown robot orientation rotates clockwise by that many 60-degree steps as rolled on the die. Throw damage is always equal to the throw strength.
  \item \textbf{Separation:} If robots are still touching after contact resolution, both robots are moved back a full unit to separate them.
  \item \textbf{Wall Collisions:} Driving into a wall stops the robot without a throw, but any contacting weapon takes feedback equal to half its weapon strength, rounded down. Robots pushed into walls are thrown with throw strength equal to the largest drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} value the other robot chose, take weapon feedback if a weapon hits the wall, and take throw damage equal to throw strength.
  \item \textbf{Feedback Backward Propagation:} Reverse flow from component back into power/pressure suppliers, distributed equally (rounding up).
\end{itemize}
\end{summarybox}

\subsection{Rules Clarifications}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt, topsep=1pt, parsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Ground Game \& Wedges while Raised:} A raised robot loses the double absorption of its wedges and cannot inflict the raised status. When both robots would be raised in the same contact, randomly roll a die to determine which one is raised (that one is raised and the other is not).
  \item \textbf{Overhead Weapons:} An Overhead weapon targets a random component along the contact ray rather than the single outermost component. If the target robot has an active upright \textit{Titanium Top Plate}, it is hit instead, even if not on the ray. Overhead weapons cause no throw on the target, but the attacking weapon still receives recoil feedback normally.
  \item \textbf{Trigger Weapons:} A weapon with \textit{Trigger} does not attack if the opposing robot's active weapon hits anywhere, unless the robots remain in contact after that contact resolves. If both combatants have Trigger weapons, both will attack. A Trigger weapon that does not attack does not suffer recoil feedback.
  \item \textbf{Crushing Claw:} Following a successful hit with the Crushing Claw, on the next turn the target moves along with this robot during the movement phase as if in inertial contact and as if raised (contributing zero motion). On the subsequent turn, the claw is inactive due to its \textit{Reset} keyword.
\end{itemize}

\section{Game Formats}

You can play with any combination of construction format and combat format.
Currently the most tested playstyle is preconstucted solo play, but adjust to your
playgroup size and preferences.

\subsection{Construction Formats}
Here are the options for how to arrange building your robot.
Constructed works well if you all spend time preparing in advance,
limited works well as a more all in 1 board game type experience, 
and preconstructed is a good way to get straight into combat.

The default weight limit is 40 and default budget is \$50.
Feel free to set a different limits, but that is the number used for the automata and therefore preconstructed robots. 

\subsubsection{Constructed}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt, topsep=0.5pt, parsep=0pt]
  \item Each player brings a build deck up to the budget chosen.
  \item Each player should already have a strong idea of how they want to build their robot from that deck.
  \item You each build a robot from that within the weight budget and fight with those.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Limited}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt, topsep=0.5pt, parsep=0pt]
  \item There is a pool in the middle of cards, split into basic (zero and 1 cost) and special (the rest).
  \item You can grab anything from the basic pile as you like, and there is considered to be an infinite supply of them (proxy if needed).
  \item For special cards, there is a market of 9 face-up cards you can take from, after which you must reveal the next card from the special card deck. You cannot return any card you have taken to the supply once you have taken it.
  \item You cannot take more total cost than the budget.
  \item From this pool, you build your robot to fight with.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Preconstructed}

If you just want to dive right into the action, you can grab one of the automaton robot sheets and play using that (in this case, ignore the automation lines).
This is a good option for your first game, or if you need to skip building cause a player doesn't have a deck/turned up late.

\subsection{Combat Formats}
These are the different ways you can arrange the fights between robots.
For any multi-round format, any components destroyed in previous rounds are discarded and cannot be used in future rounds.
There is an opportunity to modify your robot between rounds, but you cannot add new components or change the chassis.


\subsubsection{Exhibition}
The simplest way to play, you just play a single battle and see who wins.
With more than 2 players you can have a free-for-all or play multiple games starting with 
the robot fresh each time.

\subsubsection{Grudge Match}
If playing with 2 players and you want more of a challenge, play this.
You play a series of the best out of 3 fights using your robots.

\subsubsection{Tournament Play}
For the authentic combat robotics experience, play a tournament.
You create a 8-player bracket using your players and fill any gaps with automata.
Then you play 3 rounds where players play against an opponent at the same win record. 

\subsubsection{Solo Play}
If you want to play on your own, try creating a robot that can then beat a gauntlet of 5 automata robots.
Shuffle the automata sheets and play against them one at a time, with destroyed components needing replacing and modification available between rounds.

\section{Automaton Robots}

There are some automaton robots which come with a pre-built setup printed on their A3 sheet. You can use dry-erase markers to write on this damage during a fight.

They will take actions according to the table written on the right-hand side of their sheet.
First check the chart to see which table to roll on based on the current state of the robot. Automata consider themselves at full spin unless they have an active weapon with spin up that is not at max spin (e.g. if their weapons are down or at max spin, they roll on the full spin / charged table).
Then roll a d6 to see which action they take.
The possible actions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt, topsep=0.5pt, parsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Rush:} They will move at maximum speed towards their opponent.
  \item \textbf{Face:} They rotate on the spot to face their opponent.
  \item \textbf{Retreat:} They face towards their opponent and move backwards 1 step.
  \item \textbf{Evade:} Move forwards and perpendicular to the line between the robots. If both directions are clear, choose at random.
\end{itemize}
From this you can calculate the drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} values that they will choose (for each they consider the current position of their opponent). 
Roll this after deciding your own drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM} values.

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent\textbf{Automaton Power Allocation:}\\
If an automaton robot has insufficient supply to power all connected components, it chooses which components to supply in order: self-righting mechanisms (if it is inverted), then weapons, then drive \inlineIcon{\resourceM}.
