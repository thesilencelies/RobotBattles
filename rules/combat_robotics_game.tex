\section{Introduction}

\textbf{Robot Battles} is a game about combat robotics. Build a robot within your budget and weight limit, then defeat your opponents in a tournament of destruction.

\section{Card Anatomy}

Cards represent the physical components, electronics, mechanical assemblies, and weapons installed onto your robot chassis.

The anatomy of each card consists of the following key attributes:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=!, labelwidth=2.8cm, font=\bfseries\color{primaryDark}]
  \item[Price (\$\$):] You cannot have more total price in your pool than your budget.
  \item[Weight:] Your robot's total weight must be within the weight budget.
  \item[Requirements:] A component is only active if its requirements are supplied by connected components. (Power, spin, pressure are the resources that need supply.)
  \item[Durability:] How much damage a component can take before destruction.
  \item[Absorption:] How much damage a component absorbs to reduce throw strength received during an attack (also absorbs feedback damage).
  \item[Outputs:] What resources the component supplies. Also includes drive.
  \item[Ability Text:] Any special rules or passive traits for the component.
\end{description}

\vspace{2pt}
\noindent\textbf{Weapon Cards:} Weapon cards will have damage in their output zone, and a template marker indicating which template the component uses and where it attacks (Figure~\ref{fig:card_weapon}).

\section{Terminology \& Keywords}

\subsection{Resource Types}

Components produce, require, and consume various resource types. The primary resources used in the game are:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{c l X}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Icon} & \textbf{Name} & \textbf{Description} \\
  \midrule
  \inlineIcon{\resourceE} & \textbf{Energy}   & Electrical power generated by batteries, switched by ESCs, and powering motors. \\
  \inlineIcon{\resourceS} & \textbf{Spin}     & Rotational kinetic power produced by weapon motors and transferred via belts/pulleys. \\
  \inlineIcon{\resourceP} & \textbf{Pressure} & Pneumatic or hydraulic power for actuation and lifters/flippers. \\
  \inlineIcon{\resourceM} & \textbf{Drive}    & Motive force delivered to wheels or tracks for arena mobility. \\
  \inlineIcon{\resourceW} & \textbf{Damage}   & Destructive impact force dealt to opposing components during active contact. \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\subsection{Keywords}

Components may have keywords that define specific mechanical rules and interactions:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.10}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{p{3.8cm} X}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Keyword} & \textbf{Summary} \\
  \midrule
  \keywordbadge{kw_fragile}{Fragile} & If this component takes weapon damage it is destroyed as if it had a durability of 1 \\
  \keywordbadge{kw_spin_up}{Spin up X} & Add a spin counter at turn start (max X); cleared on attack, hitting a wall or becoming inactive. \\
  \keywordbadge{kw_forks}{Forks} & If the opposing robot is in line with the forks during contact, they become raised for this contact (does not work while inverted)  \\
  \keywordbadge{kw_wedge}{Wedge} & If this component makes contact, the opponent becomes raised for this contact and this component has double absorption against weapon contact (does not work while inverted) \\
  \keywordbadge{kw_invertible}{Invertible} & Component functions normally while inverted. \\
  \keywordbadge{kw_self_right}{Self-right} & Inverts upright at turn end without contact if active. \\
  \keywordbadge{kw_reset}{Reset} & This weapon is inactive the turn after it is used. (this does not cause the robot to be considered knocked out if it is also a self righter) \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\section{Robot Construction}

Each robot is built onto a chassis. A chassis is a large mat that shows the robot's shape and has a weight, cost, and flip strength. It may have an ability. All cards on the robot must be on the chassis.

\begin{itemize}
  \item The chassis determines the model used for play.
  \item A card is considered connected to any other card it is touching.
  \item You may submit up to two robots (e.g., clusterbots); the total weight across all robots must be within the weight budget, and their combined cost must be within the budget. A clusterbot team is knocked out only when both robots are knocked out.
  \item Each robot must have active drive to be submitted.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Resource Supply and Split Supply}

A component is only active if its requirements are supplied by connected components. If multiple components supply multiple components, sum up and distribute available supply across connected components. When there is not enough supply to power all connected components, the player chooses which components get supplied during the planning phase each turn.

\section{Battle Rules}

\subsection{Match Length \& Win Conditions}

The battle is played for \textbf{20 rounds} or until a robot is knocked out. A robot is knocked out if it has no active drive and no way to uninvert itself to regain drive (a self-righting weapon temporarily inactive due to \textit{Reset} does not cause the robot to be considered knocked out). A robot pushed or thrown into a marked Hazard Wall is also immediately knocked out.

If neither robot is knocked out by the end of round 20, the winner is decided by a \textbf{Judge's Decision}. The judge's decision damage count is the number of cards destroyed followed by the number of cards damaged (the robot with fewer cards destroyed wins; if tied, the robot with fewer cards damaged wins; if still tied, the match is a draw).

\subsection{Round Structure}

Each round follows a sequence of four phases:

\noindent
{\small\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l p{12.5cm}}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Phase} & \textbf{Summary} \\
  \midrule
  \textbf{1. Planning} & Players secretly select drive settings for each side (absolute value $\le$ active drive count). If supply is insufficient to power all components, players also choose which components to supply. \\
  \textbf{2. Movement} & Choices are simultaneously revealed; robots advance along the movement templates. \\
  \textbf{3. Collision} & Collisions and contacts (active weapon hits or inert pushes) are resolved. \\
  \textbf{4. Cleanup}  & Reset temporary states, verify active drive, and proceed to the next round. \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabularx}}

\clearpage

\subsection{Movement Execution}

Movement units are found on the straight movement template. Where an effect says a robot moves that amount, move them along that template without changing facing.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[height=4.3cm]{images/movement_templates.png}
    \caption{Movement Templates}
    \label{fig:movement_templates}
\end{figure}

\begingroup
\emergencystretch=1.5em
\begin{enumerate}[label=\alph*., leftmargin=*, noitemsep, topsep=0.5pt]
      \item In planning, players secretly choose drive settings with absolute value $\le$ active drive count on each side (\texttt{0/0} is stationary). Choices are revealed simultaneously.
      \item Both robots advance along the template corresponding to their chosen drive pair.
      \item \textbf{Forward Alignment \& Stop Lines:} Front bumper starts flush at black baseline. On curved templates, target stop lines are drawn at the robot's intended bumper angle; align the bumper flush against the target line to achieve the correct position and facing.
      \item \textbf{Left/Right Symmetry (Flipping):} Templates are printed for right turns ($L \ge R$). If turning left ($R > L$), flip the template face-down.
      \item \textbf{Reverse Maneuvers (Rear Placement):} If drive is negative, place the template baseline against the \textbf{back} (rear) of the robot; move backward to the target line.
      \item \textbf{Asymmetric Drive with a Negative:} When drive choices have opposite signs with unequal magnitudes (e.g., \texttt{3/\allowbreak-1} or \texttt{3/\allowbreak-2}), resolve the movement in two steps: first spin on the spot using the Spin Disc for the shared magnitude, then from that new heading use the Pivot Fan for the remainder.
      \item \textbf{Movements Beyond the Template:} If drive exceeds markings (e.g., $5/0$, $5/-5$, or boosted drive), move to the template end, then place another template for the remainder.
      \item \textbf{Path Overlap \& Collision Check:} Overlapping paths cause collisions at contact.
\end{enumerate}
\endgroup

\subsection{Movement Examples}

\vspace{0.2em}
\noindent\textbf{1. Straight Driving and Reverse Maneuvers (\texttt{3/3} vs \texttt{-2/-2})}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, noitemsep, topsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{(\texttt{3/3}):} Align Straight Template baseline flush against the \textbf{front bumper}. Advance straight forward until the front bumper aligns with the blue \textbf{L3 R3} line. Heading is unchanged.
  \item \textbf{(\texttt{-2/-2}):} Negative drive moves backward. Align baseline flush against the \textbf{rear bumper} pointing backward. Reverse until the rear bumper aligns with \textbf{L2 R2}. Heading is unchanged.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.2em}
\noindent\textbf{2. Curved Arcs and Left/Right Flipping (\texttt{2/1} vs \texttt{1/2})}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, noitemsep, topsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{(\texttt{2/1}):} Place the Medium Curve Template face-up with baseline at the front bumper. Advance along the curve until the front bumper aligns with line \textbf{L2 R1}.
  \item \textbf{(\texttt{1/2}):} Turning left. Take the Medium Curve Template and \textbf{flip it face-down}. Advance along the arc to line \textbf{L2 R1}, sweeping counter-clockwise.
\end{itemize}

\section{Collisions \& Contact Resolution}

\subsection{Determining the Contact Point}

For a collision, place both robots as far along their path as they can go without touching the other's path, then slowly move both forwards until they touch each other. Where the robots touch shows the contact point. Align the contact line from the template with the chassis and follow along that line into the robot's card layout to determine which component is contacted. Where ambiguous, both components make contact.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Active Contact:} If the parts of either robot that is touching is the weapon's active area (the red zone on the template), then the contact is active.
  \item \textbf{Inert Contact:} Otherwise, it is an inert contact.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Inert Contact (Pushing Match)}

Essentially a pushing match between colliding chassis surfaces:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Move along templates until contact.
  \item Displace the remainder of robot A's template to the end of robot B's template.
  \item Move both robots along the straight line from the initial contact point to the end of A's displaced template (a raised robot's motion does not contribute).
  \item If a robot is pushed into a wall, it is thrown with throw strength equal to the remaining distance of the move. If its weapon contacts the wall, it loses all spin counters and takes feedback $\lfloor \frac{\text{weapon strength} + \text{throw strength}}{2} \rfloor$. The robot takes throw damage equal to the throw strength.
  \item If the robots are still touching, both robots are moved back a full unit to separate them.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Active Contact (Weapon Hits)}

When weapons collide with an opponent's chassis or components:
\begin{itemize}
  \item All weapons involved in the contact are assumed to hit, and they deal damage according to their damage level to the single outermost component contacted (damage does not penetrate inwards).
  \item Then both robots are thrown away from the contact. The base throw strength received is equal to the attack damage minus the absorption of the hit component (minimum 0). Any additions to throw strength are added after that.
  \item Thrown distance is $\min(\text{d6}, \text{throw strength})$. Thrown robot orientation after active contact is decided by throwing a dice and rotating that many $60^\circ$ steps clockwise (using the numbered ticks on the rim of the Spin Disc template).
  \item Weapons receive feedback equal to half the combined attack strength in the contact. A weapon contacting an arena wall loses all spin counters and takes feedback $\lfloor \frac{\text{weapon strength} + \text{throw strength}}{2} \rfloor$.
  \item If after active contact the robots are still in contact, proceed with inertial contact.
\end{itemize}

\begin{summarybox}{Contact Classification Quick Reference}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l X}
  \textbf{Contact Type} & \textbf{Resolution Mechanism} \\
  \midrule
  \textbf{Active Contact} & Weapon strikes component $\rightarrow$ Deals damage to outermost component contacted $\rightarrow$ Base throw strength equals attack damage minus absorption of hit component (min 0) plus additions $\rightarrow$ Weapons take feedback equal to half combined attack strength $\rightarrow$ Thrown distance is $\min(\text{d6}, \text{strength}) \rightarrow$ Thrown robot orientation rotates d6 $\times 60^\circ$ clockwise $\rightarrow$ Throw damage equals throw strength. If robots are still in contact, proceed with inertial contact. \\
  \addlinespace[3pt]
  \textbf{Inert Contact} & Pushing match $\rightarrow$ Displace remainder of A's template to end of B's $\rightarrow$ Move along straight line from contact to end of displaced template $\rightarrow$ Wall collision causes throw (takes throw damage equal to throw strength) $\rightarrow$ If still touching, move both back a full unit to separate them. \\
\end{tabularx}
\end{summarybox}

\section{Combat Resolution, Damage \& Status Effects}

\subsection{Throws \& Inversion}

A robot that is thrown is moved a distance equal to $\min(\text{d6}, \text{throw strength})$ away from the contact point along the center-to-center line.
Robot orientation after active contact is decided by throwing a dice and rotating that many $60^\circ$ steps clockwise (using the numbered ticks on the rim of the Spin Disc template).
If the throw strength minus the thrown distance is greater than the flip value of the chassis, the robot is \textbf{inverted} (lower rolled distances represent high-arching vertical throws rather than horizontal displacement).
After the throw, the thrown robot takes throw damage at a randomly chosen component equal to the throw strength.
If the robot is pushed or thrown into a marked \textbf{Hazard Wall}, it is \textbf{immediately knocked out}.
The component is chosen randomly by dropping a die onto the robot and seeing which component it lands on.

\subsection{Damage Resolution}

Weapon strike damage applies only to the single outermost component contacted (internal components are damaged via throw shock):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Component Destroyed:} A component is destroyed if the damage level that hits it is equal to or greater than its durability.
  \item \textbf{Component Damaged:} A component is damaged if the damage level that hits it is equal to or greater than half its durability.
  \item \textbf{Repeated Damage:} If a damaged component would be damaged again, it is instead destroyed.
  \item \textbf{Absorption on Destruction:} If a component is destroyed, it absorbs damage equal to its durability instead of its absorption.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Feedback}

Feedback represents components over-straining themselves under combat loads. It is damage that progresses back from the source component 
to each component that supplies it, shared equally if there are multiple components (rounding up), down the tree until the damage is all absorbed.

\subsection{Status Effects}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=!, labelwidth=2.5cm, font=\bfseries\color{primaryDark}]
  \item[Inverted:] While a robot is inverted, weapons, drive, and component abilities (such as wedges and forks) without the \textit{invertible} keyword don't work. If an inverted robot would be inverted again, it becomes the right way up.
  \item[Raised:] Some components (mostly forks) will make the opponent raised during contact if any part of the opposing robot is in line with them. A robot that is raised has its flip strength halved, has the throw strength of its attacks reduced by 2, and its motion does not contribute to the motion in inertial contact.
\end{description}

\begin{summarybox}{Damage \& Feedback Rules Summary}
\small
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=1.5pt, topsep=1pt, parsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Direct Weapon Damage:} Weapon strike damage applies only to the single outermost component hit; excess damage does not penetrate inwards (throw shock covers internals).
  \item \textbf{Damage Conditions:} Damage $\ge$ durability $\implies$ \textbf{Destroyed}. Damage $\ge \frac{1}{2}$ durability $\implies$ \textbf{Damaged}. Absorption reduces base throw strength received during attacks.
  \item \textbf{Throw Distance \& Damage:} Thrown distance is $\min(\text{d6}, \text{throw strength})$. Thrown robot orientation rotates d6 $\times 60^\circ$ clockwise. Throw damage is always equal to the throw strength.
  \item \textbf{Separation:} If robots are still touching after contact resolution, both robots are moved back a full unit to separate them.
  \item \textbf{Wall Collisions:} Driving into a wall stops the robot without a throw, but any contacting weapon takes feedback $\lfloor \frac{\text{weapon strength}}{2} \rfloor$. Robots pushed into walls are thrown with throw strength equal to remaining distance, take weapon feedback if a weapon hits the wall, and take throw damage equal to throw strength.
  \item \textbf{Feedback Backward Propagation:} Reverse flow from component back into power/pressure suppliers, distributed equally (rounding up).
\end{itemize}
\end{summarybox}

\section{Game Formats}

\subsection{Robot Weight Limits}
The default weight limit is 40. Feel free to set a different weight limit for your own play, but that is the number used for the automata. All robots must be built within the weight budget.

\subsection{Preconstructed}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt, topsep=0.5pt, parsep=0pt]
  \item Each player brings a build deck up to the budget chosen (we recommend \$50).
  \item Then you each build a robot from that within the weight budget and fight with those.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limited (Recommended)}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt, topsep=0.5pt, parsep=0pt]
  \item There is a pool in the middle of cards, split into basic and special.
  \item You can grab anything from the basic pile.
  \item For special cards, there is a market of 9 face-up cards you can take from, after which you must reveal the next card. You cannot return any card you have taken to the supply once you have taken it.
  \item You cannot take more total cost than your budget (\$50 recommended).
  \item From this pool, you build your robot to fight with. You are considered to have an infinite supply of all free components.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Quickstart / Latecomer}

If you just want to dive right into the action, you can grab one of the automaton robot sheets and play using that (in this case, ignore the automation lines).

\subsection{Tournament Play}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt, topsep=0.5pt, parsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Format Structure:} Robot Battles is designed to be played across multiple battles (typically 3), where you repair your robot from the spares in your supply between rounds. Components destroyed in one round cannot be used in future rounds and must be discarded. Damaged components can be repaired between rounds from your supply.
  \item \textbf{Victory Condition:} The winner of the tournament is the player with the most wins after all games are played.
  \item \textbf{Pairings:} Battles are played with random pairings for round 1, then with a ``winners play winners'' pattern. If you have an odd number of players, include an automaton as one person in the tournament (feel free to include more if you like).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Automaton Robots}

There are some automaton robots which come with a pre-built setup printed on their A3 sheet. You can use dry-erase markers to write on this damage during a fight.

They will take actions according to the table written on the right-hand side of their sheet.
First check the chart to see which table to roll on based on the current state of the robot. Automata consider themselves at full spin unless they have an active weapon with spin up that is not at max spin (e.g. if their weapons are down or at max spin, they roll on the full spin / charged table).
Then roll a d6 to see which action they take.
The possible actions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt, topsep=0.5pt, parsep=0pt]
  \item \textbf{Rush:} They will move at maximum speed towards their opponent.
  \item \textbf{Face:} They rotate on the spot to face their opponent.
  \item \textbf{Retreat:} They face towards their opponent and move backwards 1 step.
  \item \textbf{Evade:} Move forwards and perpendicular to the line between the robots. If both directions are clear, choose at random.
\end{itemize}
From this you can calculate the drive values that they will choose (for each they consider the current position of their opponent). 
Roll this after deciding your own drive values.

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent\textbf{Automaton Power Allocation:}\\
If an automaton robot has insufficient supply to power all connected components, it chooses which components to supply in order: self-righting mechanisms (if it is inverted), then weapons, then drive.
